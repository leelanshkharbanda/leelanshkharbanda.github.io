HPC-Boost v2: Choosing Four Counters, Then Saying No — Research record · v2 team size unverified

A version-qualified research case study of fixed-subset counter selection, grouped validation, and the decision not to add a learned routing layer.

Question

01 / 05

Can four CPU performance-counter events separate malware from benign workloads without leaking binary identity into validation?

Mechanism

MOVING PARTS

Thirteen guest PMU events were cycled in separate-boot groups of at most four, anchored on cycles. Six marginal statistics per event gave 78 features, or 24 per four-event subset. All C(13,4) = 715 subsets were trained with an XGBoost detector of 100 trees at depth 4. Inner cross-validation selected the subset and tuned the threshold at TPR 0.95 while five outer folds held out whole binaries. A routing ladder compared one global subset against per-family, per-binary and per-run oracles; held-out binaries never chose the subset.

This is a version-qualified research record. The two-person resume line is version-unspecified and the imported earlier report names different authors, so no team size is applied to v2 or to the repository. Contribution is described as shared team work.

Evidence & source

EVIDENCE · CHECKED 2026-11-26

E-01 · Mixed evidence
A version-qualified corpus result is reported. Value: 45 binaries / 225 runs, F1 0.889, balanced accuracy 0.886, FPR 0.174. Conditions: five outer folds held out whole binaries; one detector family (XGBoost, 100 trees, depth 4) and one operating point (threshold tuned at TPR 0.95) on a corpus of 22 malware binaries from only Tsunami and Kaiji families.
E-02 · Simulation result
Per-binary conditioning added almost nothing. Value: +0.0045 balanced accuracy from the per-binary oracle against one global subset. Conditions: corpus-specific; the decision was not to build a learned routing layer.
