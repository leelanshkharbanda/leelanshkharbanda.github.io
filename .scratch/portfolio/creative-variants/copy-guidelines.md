# Personal Showcase Copy Guidelines

## Voice

Curious, first-person, specific, a little playful. Sound like someone showing a friend what they made—not a consultant pitching an engagement.

## Prefer

- “Hi, I'm Leelansh. I make simulations, tools, and hardware-ish experiments because I like knowing how things work.”
- “Stuff I've made”
- “Pick a project and poke around”
- “What I'm poking at”
- “See the code”
- “Try the live demo”
- “About me”
- “Say hi”

## Avoid

- “Evidence before spectacle”
- “Selected work” as the dominant personality
- “I build inspectable systems across software and hardware” as the main hero
- Services, capabilities, engagements, clients, rates, availability, or seniority positioning
- Generic “passionate engineer” language
- Hype words: innovative, seamless, production-ready, scalable, impactful

## Project-card voice examples

- Chronos-DSP: “I wanted to see what happens between a transmitter and a receiver, so I built a tiny wireless world.”
- Life OS: “A desktop app that tries to remember your life without shipping everything to a server.”
- Modulation Studio: “A signal lab for breaking waveforms and watching what happens.”
- MoE Memory Wall: “A simulation for hiding expert-weight loads when the GPU's memory gets crowded.”

## Facts stay intact

Team credit, simulation/measurement labels, source links, limits, and project-specific technical details remain visible in project records. Personal tone can simplify the front door, not the evidence.
