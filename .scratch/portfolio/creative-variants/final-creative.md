# Final Creative Direction: Scrapbook-led, Playground-powered

## Verdict

Advance **Personal Scrapbook** as the visual and narrative base. Graft only two bounded Playground strengths: its accessible SignalToy and its project-specific disclosure pattern. Do not average the three systems into a new theme.

Review basis: current branch sources plus 18 equivalent desktop/mobile captures rendered from the existing builds. The requested `test-results/*-v2.png` files were absent from all three worktrees.

Standalone branches, scored on the requested creative axes. Index weights: personality/authorship 40%, visual originality 30%, emotional fit 30%. Payload is excluded.

## Why Scrapbook wins

It sounds like a person thinking aloud. “Things I’ve built, broken & learned,” “Still figuring out how things work,” and insights about two renderers disagreeing or local-first becoming messy are specific and unguarded. The revised homepage introduction also replaces the old capabilities inventory with a credible first-person hook.

Its identity is structural, not cosmetic: corkboard geometry, ruled paper, taped polaroids, asymmetric placement, and annotations form one authored world. The second pass fixes the mobile clipping with a true single-column board and removes mobile card rotation without flattening the desktop composition.

Most importantly, it invites rather than impresses. Warm paper and visible rough edges make failed experiments feel shareable. Questions, limits, credit, evidence labels, and source routes remain present; the compact secondary homepage cards now improve comparison.

## Precise combination

1. **Keep Scrapbook’s home/work/about architecture and art direction.** Retain the notebook hero, corkboard, compact secondary cards, margin-note voice, and mobile reflow.
2. **Place Playground’s SignalToy inside the lead Chronos-DSP card as an optional “move one wall” experiment.** Preserve horizontal-only dragging, native ranges, keyboard arrows/Home/End, reset, reduced-motion behavior, the “sketch, not measurement” boundary, and a direct project link. It must not become a second hero.
3. **Borrow Playground’s per-project disclosure pattern, not its card styling.** Use Scrapbook-specific labels and revelations; expose live-demo actions beside source routes where available. A simple jump-link strip may sit above the Work board, but the cards stay scrapbook objects.
4. **Finish the voice pass.** Replace “relevant role” and anti-marketing declarations with invitations such as “Send a question or a useful rabbit hole.”

## Rejected directions

**Reject Project Playground as the final direction.** Its new hero and SignalToy are strong, but rounded bordered cards, hard shadows, sticker stacks, and familiar toolkit/archive grids still read as a tactile reskin of the baseline. “No polished sales pitch,” “Meet the human,” and “Start with the problem, not the pitch” announce non-corporate personality instead of simply demonstrating it; About still drifts toward recruiting. Keep it as an interaction donor, not the identity.

**Reject After-Dark Arcade wholesale.** The cabinet selector and compact comparison are clever, and it has the highest formal originality, but “Player 01,” class, loadout, side quests, bonus levels, and “Meet the player” put Leelansh in a costume. Neon/HUD/scanline language is recognizable genre styling rather than personal authorship. The decorative signal field still does not explain a project, and the dark technical atmosphere feels cooler and more remote than showing a friend what you made. Do not import the cabinet, companion cursor, glitch treatment, player card, or arcade copy.

## Final backlog

- Replace generic “Margin note” labels with project-specific insight headings; keep demo/source routes visible.
- Trim repeated summaries, bring the first project higher, and remove recruiting/anti-pitch language.
- Regenerate branch-correct `test-results/*-v2.png` artifacts before handoff.
